# Sales / Business Development Tear Sheet

## Purpose
This is a briefing for a rep heading into a meeting with the company. They need to know what it does, what leadership is focused on now, and how to connect to it. It is the most narrative of the four: it reads like a memo, not a data sheet. Write in plain language, so a non-finance reader understands every sentence.

**Length:** about 900–1,200 words. Favour readability over density, but a rep reads this in ten minutes before a meeting, so cut anything that doesn't help in the room.

## Source plan
Log each result to `tear-sheet-work/data.csv`. See `sources.md`.

1. **Profile and scale**: what it sells, to whom, where, headcount, latest annual revenue and prior year, gross margin (public: 10-K or release; private: reputable reports, labelled).
2. **Strategy**: the latest earnings release and call (CEO priorities, guidance, initiatives), investor-day materials, the CEO's recent letters or interviews. For private companies, use press releases and interviews.
3. **Relationships and news**: customers, vendors and technology partners named publicly, competitors, recent deals, launches, leadership changes, and upcoming dated events.

Three passes are usually enough. Sparse results for a private company are fine; synthesize what exists.

## Sections (priority order)

1. **Header and lead**: name, ticker or "Private company", HQ, industry, employees, latest annual revenue (rounded), market cap if public, guidance headline if any. Lead sentence: who they are and what they care about right now.
2. **Company overview** (the most important section): 3–5 plain sentences on what they do, who buys, how they make money, where they operate, and what makes them distinctive.
3. **Strategic priorities**: 3–5 themed bullets, each with a bold label, then the evidence with a number or date ("**International growth:** Europe reached 25% of revenue, up from 18%"). These are themes, not an earnings recap. For private companies, use "Recent developments" instead.
4. **Financial snapshot**: at most three rows (revenue, gross margin, employees) across two years plus change. No EV, multiples or balance sheet.
5. **Key relationships and ecosystem**: customers, vendors/technology, partners and competitors, 3–5 each, every entry with a parenthetical saying what the relationship is.
6. **Recent news and coming up**: 3–5 dated one-line bullets, newest first, led by the latest earnings. Then a **Coming up:** line listing dated upcoming events, which are natural reasons to reach out.
7. **Conversation starters** (cut first; labelled as suggestions): 2–3 questions, each tagged with a persona (CTO, CFO, VP Ops…). Each one cites a specific priority, number, product or date from above. If the user said what they sell or who they're meeting, tailor to that. A question that could apply to any company in the sector doesn't qualify.
8. **Sources.**

# Fund types: what matters, sections, skeptic's pass

Every tear sheet starts with the **Snapshot** then **Allocations** (`allocation.md`), and ends with **Alternatives** and **Sources**; the type decides the middle. Snapshot (all types): ticker and share class, issuer, structure, inception, total net assets (and share-class assets), expense ratio (gross/net, waiver end), benchmark, price or NAV with date, the relevant yield with its type and date, number of holdings, turnover, minimum investment if a mutual fund, and Morningstar category/rating if found (attributed, dated). Lead sentence: what this fund is in one line plus the single most important fact for an investor ("A $400B+ Nasdaq-100 tracker whose top 10 holdings are about half the fund").

**Alternatives** (all types): 2–4 funds an investor would plausibly compare — cheaper share class or twin of the same portfolio, the closest rival, the plain-vanilla broad-market version — as a table: fund · ticker · expense ratio · what's different. Facts only; never "you should switch". Use `fund_calc.py` for the fee difference in dollars.

## Index equity (QQQ, VOO, VUG, VTI)
- **Questions:** What index, and how is it built? How concentrated? How well does it track? What does it cost vs. near-identical funds?
- **Sections:** Snapshot · The index (methodology in 2–3 sentences: eligibility, weighting, caps, rebalancing — from the index provider) · What it holds (top 10 table with weights; sector mix using the issuer's own classification, named (GICS, ICB); top-10 share and effective number of holdings from fund_calc) · Performance (1/3/5/10y annualized, NAV and benchmark, same end date; calendar-year returns in Detail; worst drawdown if sourced) · Costs and tracking (expense ratio, tracking difference vs index, ETF premium/discount and typical spread, cheaper twin) · Risks · Alternatives · Sources.
- **Structural or fee changes:** if the fund changed structure, index or fee, say when, and read performance and tracking difference from before the change as the old fund's record.
- **Skeptic's pass:** concentration (a few mega-caps dominating; rule-driven caps or special rebalances); what the index excludes (e.g. Nasdaq-100 excludes financials and non-Nasdaq listings — a sector bet, not "the market"); overlap with broad funds the investor may already own; cheaper twin share class (QQQ vs QQQM); structure quirks — check the *current* structure in the latest prospectus rather than from memory: some older unit-investment-trust ETFs (SPY; QQQ until a reported Dec 2025 conversion) can't lend securities or reinvest dividends, and conversions change that.

## Active equity (FDCAX, growth/value mutual funds)
- **Questions:** Who manages it and how? Has it beaten its benchmark after fees, consistently? Is it really active? What does it cost, including taxes?
- **Sections:** Snapshot · Manager and process (manager names and tenure from the prospectus; process in 2–3 sentences from the annual report) · What it holds (top 10, sector vs benchmark, number of holdings, turnover) · Performance vs benchmark and category (1/3/5/10y, excess return from fund_calc; how many of the last 10 calendar years it beat the benchmark if sourced) · Costs and taxes (expense ratio vs index alternative and fee drag; capital-gains distributions in recent years as % of NAV — material in taxable accounts, irrelevant inside an IRA or 401(k); say which) · Risks · Alternatives (index fund with the same benchmark) · Sources.
- **Performance-based fees** (common at some large active-fund families): the management fee moves up or down (often by up to ±0.20 pts) with the fund's 36-month record against its benchmark, so the expense ratio differs between the prospectus, the annual report and quote sites. Report the latest prospectus figure as the reference. Explain the adjustment and show the range seen, with dates. Don't silently pick one.
- **Benchmark vs index-fund proxy:** compare with the official benchmark index returns when you can find them for the same end date. An index fund tracking that benchmark (after its own small fee) is an acceptable, labelled proxy, and is arguably the fairer comparison because it's what an investor could actually have bought.
- **Dependence on one year:** if the long-run edge comes mostly from one calendar year, show the record with and without that year (fund_calc's calendar rows give years beaten).
- **Skeptic's pass:** extra market risk masquerading as skill (beta above 1 against the benchmark; alpha after adjusting for beta, if a source publishes it); closet indexing (top holdings ≈ benchmark's; active share if published); performance concentrated in a few years or before the current manager; style drift vs stated objective; manager change; fund size growth; persistent outflows; tax drag from high turnover.

## Money market (VMFXX, SPAXX, SWVXX)
- **Questions:** What does it hold, how safe and liquid is it, what does it yield vs Treasury bills and rivals, and how is it taxed?
- **Sections:** Snapshot (7-day SEC yield with date; gross vs net; government / prime / tax-exempt classification; stable $1.00 NAV) · What it holds (Treasury bills, agency debt, repurchase agreements — % mix from the latest monthly holdings or N-MFP; WAM and WAL in days) · Yield in context (7-day SEC yield vs 3-month T-bill yield and 2–3 rival money funds, same date; how yield follows Fed rate changes) · Taxes (federal: ordinary income; state: share of income from US government obligations that may be exempt from state tax — issuer publishes it annually; note that repo income usually isn't exempt and some states have thresholds) · Risks (rate cuts lower yield quickly; the $1.00 NAV is a goal, not a guarantee; government funds have no liquidity fees by default under SEC rules — check current rules and the prospectus) · Alternatives (rival money funds; Treasury-only funds; direct T-bills) · Sources.
- **State tax:** follow the tax state under "Defaults for this installation". When one is set and `state-tax.md` exists beside this file, read it for that state's rules. When a state is set but has no such file, find from the state's tax authority whether and how it exempts fund dividends attributable to US government obligations (some states exempt any such share; a few, such as California, Connecticut and New York, only when the fund holds at least 50% of its assets in them at each quarter end), and its current marginal rates. With no state set, say that state treatment depends on the reader's state and that some states apply a 50% threshold; show no state bands. Either way: this only matters in a taxable account (inside an IRA or 401(k) the exemption is irrelevant: traditional-IRA withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income, and qualified Roth withdrawals aren't taxed at all). Report what share of income came from US government obligations for the latest tax year, with the source, or say it couldn't be found. Federal-agency debt and repo income generally don't count toward the exemption. For state bands, run fund_calc with `tax,state_exempt_pct` (0 if the fund failed a threshold test) and one `tax,state_rate` row per marginal rate of the state.
- **Holdings file:** a money fund's holdings are hundreds of short instruments, so don't build a company-style holdings.csv. Record the asset-type mix (Treasury / agency / repo) and WAM/WAL instead.
- **Skeptic's pass:** fee waivers flattering yield; yield vs T-bills after the expense ratio; state-tax angle (a Treasury-only fund can beat a higher-yielding government fund after state tax for residents of high-tax states — show the arithmetic with fund_calc's tax-equivalent bands, never assume the user's bracket).

## Bond (BND, AGG, active bond funds)
- **Questions:** How sensitive to interest rates (duration)? How much credit risk? What does it yield (SEC yield vs yield to maturity)? Cost vs alternatives?
- **Sections:** Snapshot · What it holds (sector mix: Treasury/agency MBS/corporate; credit-quality buckets; average duration and maturity) · Yield (30-day SEC yield, yield to maturity if published, distribution yield — named) · Performance (incl. worst calendar year, e.g. 2022) · Rate sensitivity (rule of thumb: price change ≈ −duration × rate change; computed by fund_calc for ±1%) · Risks · Alternatives · Sources.
- **Skeptic's pass:** duration higher than the investor may expect; "total market" weighting toward Treasuries; credit quality drift in active funds; yield chasing via lower quality.

## Target-date / allocation (VTWNX, VFIFX)
- **Questions:** What's the mix now, where is the glide path taking it, what's underneath, and what does the wrapper cost?
- **Sections:** Snapshot (target year; whether it has reached its target and its terminal allocation) · Current allocation (underlying funds with weights — stocks/bonds/US/international/TIPS/short-term) · Glide path (how the mix changes from here, from the prospectus; when it reaches its final mix) · Performance vs its composite benchmark · Costs (fund-level expense ratio vs the weighted expense ratio of the underlying funds, from fund_calc's `underlying` rows and the underlying funds' own fact sheets; run with `--fund-of-funds`) · Risks (sequence risk near the target date; inflation; equity share at retirement compared with rivals' — "to" vs "through" glide paths) · Alternatives (the same family's income fund; rival families' same-year funds with their equity share) · Sources.
- **Skeptic's pass:** equity share at and after the target date vs rivals; whether the mix suits someone who retired years ago vs the target year itself; for taxable accounts, embedded bond income and rebalancing distributions.

## Thematic or concentrated active (ARKK, sector/theme ETFs)
- **Questions:** What theme, what does it actually own, how concentrated and how volatile, how has it done across a full cycle, and does the theme justify the fee?
- **Sections:** Snapshot · Strategy and manager (stated theme, how holdings are chosen, manager/key person) · What it holds (top 10 with weights, top-10 share, effective number of holdings, overlap with QQQ/broad indexes if sourced) · Performance across the cycle (calendar-year returns including the peak and the drawdown; peak-to-trough and current distance from peak; vs a broad benchmark) · Flows and size (assets over time, notable inflows/outflows) · Costs · Risks (single-name concentration, liquidity of small holdings, key-person) · Alternatives (broad index; cheaper sector funds) · Sources.
- **Skeptic's pass:** private or illiquid holdings valued by the manager rather than by a market price (fair-value), with their weight and how they're valued; conflicting AUM figures across sites (check shares outstanding × NAV); investor (dollar-weighted) return vs fund (time-weighted) return if sourced — money arriving near the peak; drawdown depth and recovery time; holdings illiquidity relative to fund ownership; turnover.

## Leveraged / inverse (TQQQ, SQQQ)
- Daily-reset leverage: explain volatility decay with a two-day numeric example; returns over longer periods are not 2× or 3× the index. High costs (expense ratio + swap financing). Designed for short holding periods per the prospectus — quote it. Use the index-equity sections otherwise.
